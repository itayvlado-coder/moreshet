# ICP Profile — The Viewer

*Note: for Moreshet, the buyer (army units, starting with the IDF) and the audience are different people. This file profiles the audience — the person actually watching the series — because every episode has to speak their language, not military jargon. Every agent writes for this person.*

---

## Who They Are

**Situation:** A general-public viewer with no military background or expertise. They know a war happened but have no real sense of what it was like on the ground.

**Context:** Comes to the series out of curiosity, not obligation — no prior knowledge of units, ranks, or operations required or expected.

**Age range:** Broad general public.

**Location:** Primarily Hebrew-speaking (Israeli), with the door open to a wider audience over time.

---

## Their Day

Not applicable in the usual ICP sense — this is a viewer profile, not a buyer persona. They're not managing a business problem; they're choosing what to watch.

---

## Their Problem

**What they say they need:**
"I want to know what it was actually like from the inside."

**What they actually need:**
A narrative that makes sense of chaotic, real events without requiring them to already understand military structure, terminology, or tactics — the human experience over the operational facts.

**What they have already tried:**
News coverage and official army footage — both leave them with facts but no narrative, no sense of what it felt like to be there.

---

## What They Want

To understand what went on in the heads of the soldiers on the ground — and to want to finish the entire series, not just sample an episode.

---

## What Builds Their Trust

Real names, real units, real events — verified, not embellished. The feeling that this is authentic and earned, not dramatized for effect. Exclusive access (material no one else has) signals that this is the real thing, not another recap of what's already public.

---

## Their Objections

- **"War content is heavy — I might not want to sit with that."** Underneath: fear of a depressing, static viewing experience rather than a gripping one.
- **"I won't understand the military terminology."** Underneath: past experience with news/official content that assumed prior knowledge and lost them.
- **"Is this going to be dry facts or an actual story?"** Underneath: burned before by content that had access but no narrative.

---

## How to Talk to Them

Never assume military knowledge — translate jargon into human stakes. Lead with the person, not the operation. Keep pacing tight enough that "one more episode" feels natural, not effortful.
