# Voice DNA — How Moreshet Sounds

*Every agent reads this before writing anything.*

---

## Brand Personality

Cinematic and present-tense — events unfold as if happening now, not being recalled after the fact. Mythic and heroic in framing: real names, real units, real places, woven into a story with the weight of legend. Emotionally direct — it does not shy away from pain, loss, or moral complexity, and sits pain and pride side by side rather than resolving them. Grounded in camaraderie and sacrifice as the emotional core, not spectacle for its own sake. Trustworthy above all — the drama has to earn itself through real, verified material, never exaggeration.

**Four words that describe your brand:**
Exciting. Interesting. Captivating. Trustworthy.

## What We Never Do

- We never sound boring. If a scene doesn't grip, it doesn't survive the edit.
- We never sensationalize beyond what the material actually supports — trust comes first.
- We never sanitize the hard parts — loss and moral difficulty stay in the story.

---

## Writing Style

- Brief, but not too short — enough space for a scene to breathe, no more.
- Present tense, cinematic pacing — the story moves like it's happening now.
- Real names, real units, real places — specificity over abstraction.
- Pain and pride sit next to each other, unresolved — don't flatten the ending into pure triumph.

---

## Vocabulary

**Use these:**
- Concrete unit/operation names and real details as they appear in source material (in Hebrew where the material is Hebrew)

**Avoid these:**
- Generic war-movie clichés
- Sanitized or overly diplomatic phrasing that dulls the real stakes

---

## Our Recurring Themes

1. **Camaraderie under fire (רעות)** — the bond between soldiers is the emotional throughline of every episode.
2. **Resourcefulness in chaos** — commanders and soldiers improvising in real time when the plan breaks down.
3. **Sacrifice and loss, immediately followed by the next mission** — grief doesn't get to pause the story.
4. **Moral complexity** — civilians, hostages, and combatants sharing the same space; no easy answers shown as easy.
5. **Hope and rescue against long odds** — the pull toward saving hostages/civilians even when the cost is high.
